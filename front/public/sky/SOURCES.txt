Modified (all ten files are derivatives and are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0)
  - rock silhouettes extracted to an alpha mask from the photograph and recoloured flat
    (night: oklch(0.135 0.01 50), the page's --bg-deep; afternoon: oklch(0.50 0.075 36));
  - horizon straightened (linear tilt of 37 px across the frame removed);
  - star field and Milky Way isolated from the photograph (night only), dimmed under the nav,
    and re-composited on a generated OKLab sky gradient (horizon glow capped at L 0.585 so the
    cream name keeps >= 3:1);
  - a generated line of distant mesas added edge to edge (flat stepped tables, 1/f texture,
    cliffs, ledges and talus), hazier towards its base in the afternoon;
  - a generated near-plain band (2-8 px, irregular top) across the full width, the rock feet
    dissolved into it (afternoon), so the bottom row is land, never a straight cut;
  - a generated sun disc (afternoon); monochrome grain; cropped and resized.

Page contract
  The bottom edge of every file is the horizon. Use with object-fit: cover and
  object-position: 52% 100% on a box whose bottom is the horizon. The CSS ground starts on
  that same line, 1 px higher (it overlaps the file's last row), with the land colour:
    night      --ground-foot = --bg-deep = oklch(0.135 0.01 50)
    afternoon  --ground-foot = oklch(0.60 0.07 40), easing to sand over ~120 px
  Desktop files are 3.75:1 and fill boxes from about 2.6:1 to 6:1 without a gap; the skyline
  and the land band run edge to edge, so no width can show a cut. <picture> art direction:
  <= 760 px the 800/1000 files (sizes 100vw), 761-1100 px the tab1600 file, above that
  1600/2400. RTL mirrors the sky with scaleX(-1).
